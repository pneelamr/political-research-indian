# Research Standards

## Source Hierarchy

Use the strongest source appropriate to the claim rather than applying one hierarchy mechanically to every topic.

### Institutional and Primary Records

- Election Commission of India and state chief electoral officer records
- Parliament of India and state legislature records, debates, questions, member profiles, and committee reports
- Gazette notifications, statutes, rules, budgets, ministry reports, audit reports, and official statistical publications
- Supreme Court, High Court, tribunal, commission, and inquiry records
- official archives, presidential or gubernatorial notifications, and government releases
- contemporaneous speeches, interviews, manifestos, party resolutions, letters, memoirs, and autobiographies

Primary records establish what was formally recorded, enacted, counted, or said. They do not automatically establish effectiveness, motive, or historical significance.

### Independent Interpretation and Context

- peer-reviewed scholarship and books from reputable academic or trade publishers
- respected reference works and institutional biographies
- established national and regional reporting with named authors, dates, and editorial accountability
- credible specialist research organizations with transparent methods

Use scholarship and independent reporting for political context, implementation, social effects, opposition, and competing interpretations. For regional leaders, include credible sources in the relevant Indian language where useful; translate accurately and retain the original title or phrase when it matters.

### Interested or Advocacy Sources

Party websites, campaign material, supporters' accounts, opponents' claims, social-media posts, and the subject's own retrospective narrative may be valuable primary evidence. Label their standpoint. Do not use them alone to validate disputed achievements, motives, or accusations.

Wikipedia and aggregators may assist discovery but should not normally be the final citation for claims when an underlying source is available.

## Claim Handling

For each material claim, capture:

- `Claim type`: Recorded fact, Attributed position, Analysis, or Disputed/uncertain
- `Source IDs`
- `Corroboration`: Primary record, Multiple independent sources, Single credible source, Interested source only, or Unresolved
- `Research note`: conflicts, missing records, translation issues, or date ambiguity

Also retain a stable claim ID and its record/field, the precise statement, source IDs, a page/table/paragraph locator, a short inspected passage or paraphrase, access status, support/contradiction relationship, review date and review result. Use `Not reviewed`, `Supported`, `Partially supported`, `Disputed` or `Unsupported`; do not conflate these with claim type. A search snippet, abstract, inaccessible document or inherited citation must remain explicitly limited. Verify only what the inspected material actually establishes.

Separate factual policy actions, attributed objectives and analytical consequences into distinct claim units even when the presentation keeps them in one row. Split compound claims if their parts have different evidence. For migrated research, retain provisional types and candidate citations without manufacturing review history. A record can be partly reviewed; surface that status instead of transferring support from one field to its neighbours.

Record the underlying origin of each source when assessing independence. An official PDF, its mirror and a newspaper's reproduction can share a single origin. Track contradictory evidence separately and explain any resolution; do not silently clear a dispute when one preferred source has been read.

When sources disagree, do not choose silently. Record each supported version, identify the disagreement, and explain which version is used and why. Use exact dates where supported; otherwise use the most precise honest date range.

## Policy and Outcome Attribution

Use a staged model:

1. Proposal or stated objective
2. Formal adoption or enactment
3. Implementation mechanism
4. Administrative output
5. Social, political, economic, diplomatic, or institutional outcome
6. Attribution assessment

An outcome during a tenure is not automatically an achievement or failure of the office-holder. Record other actors, inherited conditions, later implementation, external shocks, and credible counterarguments. Use quantitative indicators only when the definition, geography, period, and source are clear.

## Controversies and Accountability

Create one row per distinct matter. Include:

- event and date range;
- neutral description;
- allegation or criticism and who made it;
- subject or party response, if available;
- competent authority and procedural status;
- finding, disposition, or latest status;
- political or institutional consequence;
- source IDs and dispute notes.

Do not collapse `accused`, `charged`, `convicted`, `acquitted`, and `case closed` into a generic negative label. Political criticism without legal proceedings must remain political criticism. An acquittal or absence of charges does not prove that every broader historical or ethical criticism was false; state only what the decision establishes.

## Time and Identity

Use the workbook's `As of` date for current offices, party affiliation, legal status, and living figures. Record historical party names, alliances, constituencies, state boundaries, and office titles as they existed at the time, with a normalized modern label only in a separate field when useful.

Distinguish similarly named people. Preserve common public forms such as initials while recording the full name and name variants in `Overview`.

## Gender Coverage

Research every item below for every figure. Gender may be materially important, marginal, or unsupported in a particular case; record the disposition and evidence rather than presuming the answer.

1. **Entry and advancement:** access to political organizations, recruitment, candidacy, nominations, winnable seats, leadership selection, and barriers to advancement.
2. **Milestones:** gender-related firsts, unusual offices, representative significance, and whether contemporary sources treated them as politically meaningful.
3. **Networks and pathways:** mentorship, patronage, family or dynastic entry, marriage networks, succession, and independent organization. Apply the same questions and relevance threshold to men.
4. **Role and portfolio allocation:** whether gender expectations affected ministerial portfolios, party assignments, public roles, or exclusion from security, finance, organizational, or executive authority.
5. **Campaign and media treatment:** gendered rhetoric, attacks, praise, stereotypes, appearance or dress commentary, family-role expectations, press framing, and differences from comparable male politicians.
6. **Threats and security:** harassment, sexualized abuse, threats, political violence, mobility restrictions, protective arrangements, and other security constraints when evidenced.
7. **Gender-related policy and organizations:** positions on legislation and policy affecting women or gender relations, relationships with women's organizations, and the difference between stated support, action, implementation, and outcome.
8. **Political use of identity:** whether the figure mobilized, minimized, rejected, reframed, or strategically used gender identity, and how parties, allies, opponents, or voters used it.
9. **Representation and effects:** distinguish descriptive or symbolic representation from substantive policy, institutional, organizational, or constituency effects.
10. **Assessment over time:** distinguish contemporary reception from later feminist, scholarly, journalistic, partisan, or public-memory assessments.

Do not assume that women prioritize women's issues or that gender explains conduct without evidence. Personal and family details belong only when they materially influenced recruitment, legitimacy, organization, succession, governance, conflict, public treatment, or political strategy. Apply that rule consistently across genders.

Use a relevant comparison group when making a gender-related inference: politicians in similar parties, offices, jurisdictions, and periods. A difference from all politicians across Indian history is rarely a defensible comparator.

## Era Coverage

Divide the career into phases when institutions or political conditions changed materially. Research every dimension below for each applicable phase:

1. **Party system:** Congress dominance, opposition fragmentation, regionalization, coalition politics, dominant-party competition, or another defensible configuration.
2. **Constitutional and institutional environment:** executive-legislative balance, courts, civil service, emergency conditions, civil liberties, federal relations, and strength or vulnerability of governing institutions.
3. **Electoral environment:** franchise and election rules, direct or indirect selection, constituency boundaries and delimitation, reserved seats, campaign practices, turnout conditions, and data availability.
4. **Economic regime:** planning, state ownership, nationalization, crisis management, liberalization, globalization, welfare expansion, fiscal federalism, or digital governance as applicable.
5. **International system:** decolonization, Cold War, non-alignment, post-Soviet transition, regional conflict, nuclear order, globalization, or contemporary multipolar conditions.
6. **Media and campaign environment:** print, radio, cinema, public broadcasting, satellite television, 24-hour news, mobile communication, social media, data-driven campaigning, and changes in information control or access.
7. **Political organization:** cadre, mass movement, trade union, social movement, personality-centred party, coalition network, professional campaign organization, or digital mobilization.
8. **Social environment:** caste relations and mobilization, religion and communal relations, language, regional identity, class, gender norms, urbanization, and social movements. Record only dimensions materially relevant to the career phase.
9. **State structure and political geography:** state reorganization or creation, Centre-state arrangements, local-government changes, administrative boundaries, and constituency geography.
10. **Evidence environment:** archival survival, restricted or partisan records, memoir dependence, oral history, language access, television archives, born-digital material, and the resulting uncertainty or source bias.

For each phase, identify a contemporaneous comparison group and plausible alternatives available at the time. Separate contemporaneous judgment from retrospective judgment. A `Presentism caution` should explain the risk of applying later concepts, institutions, territorial boundaries, or standards without historical adjustment; it must not excuse conduct merely because it occurred in the past.

## Coverage Review

Before authoring, test the collected evidence against these questions:

- Can a reader reconstruct the career chronologically?
- Are electoral claims traceable to official or comparably strong records?
- Are the figure's most consequential offices and political phases represented?
- Are both formal governance and party or movement politics covered?
- Are achievements tied to mechanisms and evidence rather than slogans?
- Are major failures, crises, criticism, and accountability processes treated precisely?
- Does the legacy section show who holds each interpretation and on what basis?
- Has every gender coverage item been researched and given an explicit disposition?
- Has the career been divided into meaningful eras rather than assigned one lifetime label?
- Do major claims identify the applicable political system, institutional constraints, economic and international setting, media environment, social conditions, and evidence limitations?
- Are gender comparisons made against relevant contemporaries, with symbolic and substantive representation kept distinct?
- Are contemporaneous and retrospective standards both visible, with presentism risks stated but not used as blanket excuses?
- Would a knowledgeable supporter and a knowledgeable critic recognize the factual record even if they dispute the analysis?
